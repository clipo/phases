# September 10: constructing social units

Reframed the title, English abstract, Spanish resumen, introduction, conceptual framework, discussion, and conclusion around the distinction between ceramic classifications and empirical social entities. Updated the supplement's title and scope. Numerical results, figures, and analytical methods are unchanged.

The main argument now concerns how units must be established before explaining social processes in terms of them. The distribution and movement of functional goods provide the proposed basis for evaluating functional differentiation and interdependence independently of ceramic phase membership. Spatial copying experiments support the diagnosis of ambiguous ceramic patterning; the argument does not depend on neutral drift explaining every observed contrast.

Checked the repository copy of Robert C. Dunnell's *What Is It That Actually Evolves?* (1995), particularly pp. 40–43: structure versus functional integration; requirements of evolutionary individuals; circular phase/component definitions; and interaction plus functional differentiation. The functional-goods research design is presented as our application of that argument, not as a direct quotation or as an analysis already performed. Goods movement alone is not claimed to establish an evolutionary individual, political jurisdiction, or functional integration.
